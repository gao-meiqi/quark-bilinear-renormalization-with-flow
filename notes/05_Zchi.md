# The Additional Renormalization Constant 

The flow itself does not generate new ultraviolet divergences.

The problem comes from the interaction between the flowed fermion field and the original quark field at the boundary $t_f=0$.

> **Why $Z_\psi$ is NOT enough**

Loop corrections generate additional divergences that cannot be removed by the ordinary QCD renormalization constants.

ANS2:

Conterterms!!



> **Why the Gauge Sector is Finite**

For flowed gauge fields, Lüscher showed that gauge symmetry is so
restrictive that no additional counterterm can be generated at the
boundary.

Therefore, after the ordinary renormalization of QCD,

$$
g_0 \rightarrow g_R,
$$

the flowed gauge field requires no further renormalization.

This is a highly nontrivial result and one of the major achievements of
the gradient flow formalism.



> **Why the Fermion Sector is Different**

The fermion sector is less constrained.

The symmetries of the theory allow additional boundary counterterms
associated with flowed fermion fields.

Consequently, loop corrections generate ultraviolet divergences in
correlation functions involving

$$
\chi(t,x).
$$

These divergences are not removed by the usual quark-field
renormalization constant.

Therefore an additional renormalization factor is required:

$$
\chi_R(t,x)
=
Z_\chi^{-1/2}\chi(t,x).
$$
